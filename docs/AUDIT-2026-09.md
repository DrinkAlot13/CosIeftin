## Severity 2 — WRONG DATA VISIBLE ON A PAGE

### 2.0 Price correctness — what is SOUND, stated first · SECTION 3 COMPLETE except the live fetch

Reported before the defects because it is the larger result and it is easy to lose under a list
of problems. `npm run audit:price-truth`, over **46,414 live offers**:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| **Every stored price reproduces from its own `rawPriceText`** through `parsePrice` | **46,414 of 46,414. Zero disagreements. Zero rows missing a raw string.** |
| `pricePerUnit` internally consistent (price ÷ ppu yields a real size) | 40,248 checked, **0 corrupt** |

The first line is the strongest evidence in this audit. It means no parser change has silently
rewritten history, and that every price on the site can be re-derived from the exact string the
merchant published. That is the invariant `rawPriceText` was made mandatory for, and it holds
completely.

**A correction to my own first attempt, recorded because the audit nearly invented a fault.**
The first version of this check compared `pricePerUnit` against the CATALOG's `unitSize` and
reported 232 "unit-price errors" between 1.01x and 1.06x. It was wrong: `scrape-util` computes
`ppu = price / ownSize.unitSize` — the size parsed from the **merchant's own** name. Comparing
against the catalog's size measures something else entirely. Rewritten before reporting.

### 2.1 The sitemap advertises 246 pages that return 404 · CONFIRMED

**What.** `src/app/sitemap.ts` emits `/c/<slug>` for every category that has a live offer, with
no section filter. `/c/[slug]` resolves through `getCategoryPage`, which is **grocery-only** and
calls `notFound()` for anything else.

**Evidence** (`npm run audit:sitemap`, answered from the database rather than by fetching 324
URLs):

    categories emitted into the sitemap   324
      dcneu         171  ← 404
      grocery        78  ← the only section /c/[slug] serves
      cosmetice      39  ← 404
      farmacie       31  ← 404
      alcohol         5  ← 404

    ADVERTISED BUT 404: 246 of 324 (76%)
      /c/vin  /c/whisky  /c/sampanie  /c/dcneu-absorbante  /c/cosmetice-corp  …

Confirmed independently by the rendered crawl, which hit `/c/dcneu-*`, `/c/cosmetice-*` and
`/c/farmacie-*` and got 404 with a console error on each.

**Who sees it.** Search engines, primarily — nothing in the UI links to these, so a person is
unlikely to land on one except from Google. That makes it a crawl-budget and indexing problem
rather than a broken click, which is why it is severity 2 and not 1.

**Recommended fix.** One `where` clause: the sitemap's category query should filter
`section: "grocery"`, matching what the route actually serves. The alcohol, cosmetice, farmacie
and dcneu sections have their own top-level routes (`/alcool`, `/cosmetice`, `/farmacie`,
`/dcneu`) and those *are* in the sitemap already.

**Also worth knowing:** the sitemap is a single file carrying **45,948 URLs**. Google's limit is
50,000 per file. At the current rate of catalog growth that ceiling arrives soon, and the fix
then is a sitemap index, not a smaller sitemap.

### 2.2 232 offers are matched across a DIFFERENT pack size than the catalog states · CONFIRMED

**What.** The unit price shown for an offer is computed from the **merchant's own** pack size,
while the product page states the **catalog's** size. Where the two differ, the page shows a
size and a per-unit price that do not correspond.

**Evidence** (`npm run audit:price-truth`, check A2): 232 of 40,248 (0.58%).

    by merchant: sezamo=121 carrefour=26 dcneu=24 mega-image=21 metro=21 auchan=10 freshful=7 kaufland=2

    offer 12439 [carrefour] merchant pack 0.17 kg vs catalog 0.18 kg (1.06x)  Ciocolata de menaj alba, Nestle Dessert
    offer 4359  [auchan]    merchant pack 0.10 kg vs catalog 0.095 kg (1.05x) Cafea solubila decofeinizata Nescafe
    offer 11322 [mega-image] merchant pack 1.403 l vs catalog 1.45 l (1.03x)  Balsam de rufe Coccolino Sensitive

**This is not a broken number.** It is a match made ACROSS two pack sizes, which the matcher's
±26% size tolerance permits by design. The consequence is that "cel mai mic preț pe unitate"
can compare unlike packs — a 170 g bar against a 180 g one — and the difference is invisible
because the page prints only the catalog's size.

**Recommended fix.** Not a threshold change. Show the merchant's own size in the offer row where
it differs from the catalog's, which turns a hidden discrepancy into visible information. The
data is already stored; only the rendering is missing.

### 2.3 The Kaufland unit-price oracle disagrees on 19 offers · CONFIRMED

**What.** Kaufland publishes `formattedBasePrice` — the per-unit price computed by the MERCHANT
from the real pack size. It is the only figure in the system that does not share our assumptions,
and it is one of only two external oracles this project owns.

**Evidence** (`npm run audit:unit-oracle`): 19 disagreements. The worst:

    ours        theirs
    0.00        1.14 /buc    Regalina ouă cod 2          ← WE SHOW NOTHING; they publish a price
    105.69     86.94 /kg     Dots Baton cu brânză 51 g   ← 22% high
    12.10      29.75 /l      Lenor Balsam 150 g / 504 / 1155  ← the name carries three numbers
    12.90      11.73 /kg     Covrig 100 g
    31.19      44.56 /kg     Năvodul Plin 160g

**The first row is the interesting one:** a live offer whose unit price is **0.00** while the
merchant publishes 1.14/buc. That is the `pricePerUnit = 0` shape — "size unknown" stored as a
number — appearing on a row where the merchant told us the answer and we did not read it.

**Recommended fix.** Each of the 19 needs its own look; they are size-parsing failures, not
price failures. The `Lenor … 150 g / 504 / 1155` case is a name carrying three numbers, which is
the multipack trap CLAUDE.md already documents.

### 2.4 Spread across shops, at three thresholds · CONFIRMED, a GROUP finding

Of **2,830 products priced at 2 or more shops**:

| spread | products | share |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 1.5x | 364 | 12.9% |
| ≥ 2.0x | 83 | 2.9% |
| ≥ 3.0x | 16 | 0.6% |

**This names no culprit and must not.** CLAUDE.md's peer-median limit is explicit: three false
members move the median onto themselves and the check then indicts the correct rows. The 16 at
3x are enumerated with their members and discriminating tokens by `npm run audit:wide-spread`,
and include the gelatine case CLAUDE.md documents as the worked example of exactly this trap.

**The 12.9% at 1.5x is the number worth sitting with.** One product in eight priced at two shops
disagrees by half again. Some of that is real (promotions, cash-and-carry versus retail); some is
mismatching. Nothing here can separate them, and the honest next step is reading a sample, not
tightening a threshold.

## Severity 3 — SECURITY

Every route below was fetched **with no cookies and no session** — the attacker's position, not
the browser's. `npm run audit:api` reproduces the whole table.

### 3.1 No rate limiting exists anywhere · EXPLOITABLE WHEN HOSTED

**What.** `grep -rln "rateLimit|rate-limit|throttle" src/` returns nothing. No route, middleware
or handler limits request rate.

**The concrete consequence, not the abstract one.** `POST /api/list-adds` accepts unauthenticated
writes and returns 204 to everyone. That is **deliberate and documented** — an add-count is an
anonymous aggregate, and requiring a session would measure only people with accounts, of whom
there are currently none. The design is right. What is missing is any bound on it:

    curl -X POST localhost:3000/api/list-adds -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
         -d '{"productId":1}'      → 204, unlimited, no session

`ProductAddCount.adds` feeds "L-ai adăugat în listă de N ori" on `/favorite` and the inferred-
favourites signal the optimizer reads. So anyone with `curl` can promote an arbitrary product.

**Severity.** Not exploitable for data theft; exploitable for **manipulation and cost** the
moment the site is public. On a home-hosted server `/search` is the more expensive target — it
was 880 ms before the index cache and still builds a 29,000-product index on a cold miss.

**Recommended fix.** A per-IP token bucket in middleware for the write endpoints and `/search`.
Cheap, no dependency needed for a fixed-window counter.

## Severity 4 — BROKEN UNDER LOAD OR WHEN HOSTED

### 4.0 `/sitemap.xml` takes 5.9 seconds and is regenerated per request · CONFIRMED

**Evidence** (rendered crawl): `/sitemap.xml` — **5,851 ms, TTFB 2,912 ms, 9.0 MB**. It is by far
the slowest route on the site; the next is 1,597 ms.

It builds 45,948 URLs from two unbounded Prisma queries on every request, with no caching. A
crawler hitting it repeatedly, or several at once, is 9 MB and ~3 seconds of database work each
time on a machine that is also serving shoppers.

**Recommended fix.** Cache it — it changes once a night like everything else, and `revalidate`
is the mechanism already used elsewhere. Splitting into a sitemap index becomes necessary at
50,000 URLs regardless (see 2.1).

### 4.1 Product pages cost 1.2–1.6 s on a COLD hit · CONFIRMED, with a caveat about the number

**Evidence** (rendered crawl, 43 of 310 pages over 1 s):

    /sitemap.xml   5851ms (ttfb 2912)
    /p/paste-fainoase-cuscus-monte-banato-250-g   1597ms (ttfb 1142)
    /dcneu                                        1573ms (ttfb 1562)
    /oferte                                       1501ms (ttfb 1479)
    …then a long tail of /p/ pages at 1.0–1.3 s

**The caveat matters more than the numbers.** The crawler opens a **fresh browser context per
page**, so every measurement is a first-ever hit with an empty cache. Product pages declare
`revalidate = 3600`, so the *second* visitor to the same URL gets it from cache — `probe:cold`
measured `/c/lapte` at 269 ms cold and 159 ms warm on the same server.

So this is the cost of the **first visitor to a given product page each hour**, not the typical
cost. With 45,622 product pages and a home-hosted server, that first hit is most hits — which is
why it belongs here rather than under "cosmetic". `/oferte` and `/dcneu` at ~1.5 s are the ones
worth attention first, because they are entry points rather than long-tail URLs.

### 4.2 The review queue is 96% one rule, and that rule has already decided · SECTION 7 COMPLETE

**The proposal: `mutually-distinct` should output REJECT, not REVIEW.**

**Why the queue is 73,657.** It did not grow 20x. It was switched on:

    2026-08-31    3,775     first partial day of PendingMatch persistence
    2026-09-01   49,587     first full nightly sweep across every merchant
    2026-09-08    9,054

That is before `addNew`, so `addNew` is not the cause. And the hypothesis that it is full of
different private labels is measurably wrong: pairs where BOTH sides carry a house brand and the
houses DIFFER number **9 rows — 0.0%**.

**What it actually is:**

    mutually-distinct   70,593   95.8%
    low-overlap          1,809    2.5%
    name+size              504    0.7%
    unit-price-spread      106    0.1%

`mutually-distinct` is the rule CLAUDE.md calls **decisive** — "if EACH side carries a
significant token the other lacks, they are different products". It has already concluded these
are different products. `REVIEW_THRESHOLD = 0.42` then routes 70,593 of those conclusions to a
person, asking them to second-guess the rule measured at **0% false blocks over 102 hand-read
pairs**.

**What the queue becomes: 3,064 rows** (73,657 − 70,593). That is within touching distance of the
3,775 recorded on the first day, which is a good sign that the remainder is the real queue.

**What the golden set says: NOTHING CHANGES, and this is checkable rather than hopeful.**

- `tests/golden/matching.test.ts:22` grades on `d.ok` alone — `actual: d.ok`. It never reads
  `band`.
- `scrape-util.ts:549` already returns `ok: false` for `mutually-distinct` in **both** bands. Only
  the band differs.
- The single consumer of `REVIEW` for matching is `scrape-util.ts:845` — `if (d.band !== "REVIEW")
  return;` — which decides **only** whether a `PendingMatch` row is written.

So the change alters exactly one thing: whether a row is queued. It cannot alter a match, a
price, a comparison or the golden set, which stands at **97.8% (218/223), 1 false match**.

**Not implemented, as instructed.** Two things worth deciding alongside it:

1. **`low-overlap` (1,809) has the same shape** and is not proposed for the same treatment,
   because it is a confidence statement rather than a structural conclusion. It should stay
   reviewable.
2. **0 of 73,657 rows have ever been decided.** There is therefore *no confirm rate at any band*,
   so nobody can currently calibrate `REVIEW_THRESHOLD` from evidence. Rejecting
   `mutually-distinct` is justified by the rule's own 0%-false-block measurement, not by queue
   data — and after the change, the remaining 3,064 are small enough that a real confirm rate
   becomes obtainable for the first time.

## THIS AUDIT'S OWN FALSE POSITIVES

Recorded because the brief asked whether each failure is a real defect, a deliberate record, or
an artefact of the audit — and because two of the three below were mine.

1. **"255 pages render a price with no number" — ENTIRELY MY OWN REGEX.** `EMPTY_PRICE` was
   `/(?:RON|lei)\s*(?:$|[^0-9])/`, which matches the `/` in `12,99 lei/kg`. Tested against real
   strings after the crawl:

       '12,99 lei/kg'   -> True      'de la 4,35 RON' -> True
       '8,70 lei/kg'    -> True      '— lei'          -> True

   Every normal price matches. There is **no evidence of any empty price on the site**, and the
   255 is withdrawn in full.

2. **"232 unit-price errors" — MY OWN COMPARISON.** Compared `pricePerUnit` against the catalog's
   size when it is computed from the merchant's own. Rewritten before reporting; see 2.0 and 2.2.

3. **`/shrinkflation` 404 — a deliberate record, not a defect.** Feature-flagged off with a
   comment explaining why a 404 beats an empty page.
